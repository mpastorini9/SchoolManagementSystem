# Decision Process

## Purpose

This document defines the process used to evaluate, make, and document significant decisions throughout the School Management System.

Its purpose is to ensure that important decisions are made consistently, based on clear reasoning, and properly documented for future contributors.

A documented decision process improves transparency, reduces ambiguity, and preserves project knowledge over time.

---

# Decision Principles

Important decisions should be:

- Intentional
- Well understood
- Properly documented
- Consistent with the project's vision
- Focused on long-term value

Decisions should solve real problems rather than introduce unnecessary complexity.

---

# Decision Criteria

Before making a significant decision, contributors should consider:

- The problem being solved.
- Alternative solutions.
- Benefits and trade-offs.
- Long-term impact.
- Alignment with the project's architecture and engineering principles.

Significant decisions should be based on objective reasoning whenever possible.

---

# Decision Documentation

Decisions that influence the architecture, implementation, or long-term evolution of the project should be documented.

Documentation should explain:

- The context.
- The decision.
- The rationale.
- The expected consequences.

The objective is to preserve knowledge rather than simply record outcomes.

---

# Decision Categories

Depending on their scope, decisions may be documented as:

- Architecture Decision Records (ADRs)
- Technical Decisions
- Business Decisions

The appropriate document should be selected according to the nature and impact of the decision.

---

# Reviewing Decisions

Previously documented decisions may be reviewed as the project evolves.

If new information, technologies, or business requirements justify a different approach, the original decision should remain documented while the new decision explains why the change was made.

Project history should be preserved rather than rewritten.

---

# Continuous Improvement

Decision-making should improve through experience.

Lessons learned from previous decisions should help contributors make more informed choices in the future.

Well-documented decisions strengthen both the project and its engineering culture.
