# Deprecated Decisions

## Purpose

This document defines how deprecated or superseded decisions are documented throughout the School Management System.

Its purpose is to preserve the historical context of important decisions that are no longer active while explaining why they were replaced.

Maintaining a history of deprecated decisions helps contributors understand the evolution of the project and prevents previously evaluated alternatives from being repeatedly reconsidered.

---

# When to Deprecate a Decision

A decision should be marked as deprecated when it has been intentionally replaced by a newer approach.

Examples include:

- Architectural changes.
- Technology replacements.
- Updated engineering practices.
- Revised business rules.
- Changes in product direction.

Deprecated decisions should remain documented rather than removed.

---

# Deprecation Structure

Every deprecated decision should include:

- Original Decision
- Reason for Deprecation
- Replacement Decision
- Impact
- References to Related Decisions

The objective is to preserve the project's historical knowledge while documenting its evolution.

---

# Historical Context

Deprecated decisions represent previous stages of the project's evolution.

Understanding why a decision was replaced often provides valuable context for future contributors and helps avoid repeating past mistakes.

Project history should be preserved whenever practical.

---

# Traceability

Deprecated decisions should reference the documents that replaced them.

Likewise, new decisions should reference the deprecated decisions they supersede.

Maintaining traceability improves the understanding of how the project has evolved over time.

---

# Continuous Evolution

The project is expected to evolve continuously.

Deprecating a decision should be viewed as a normal part of software evolution rather than as evidence of a poor original decision.

Well-documented changes strengthen the project's long-term maintainability.
